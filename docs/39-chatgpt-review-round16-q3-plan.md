# ChatGPT Research Review — Round 16: Q3 Research Plan & Novelty Freeze

Date: 2026-09-18  
Repository: `lathevinh/fas`, branch `main`

## 1. Verdict

**The research direction is now coherent enough to freeze as a Q3 paper plan before coding.**

I do not recommend adding another architecture, loss, foundation model, cue ontology, or routing mechanism before experiments.

The strongest version of the paper is no longer:

> DINO + VLM + disagreement.

It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{source-domain cross-fitted failure-risk estimation}
}
\]

for a fixed heterogeneous FAS classifier under strict unseen-domain evaluation.

The heterogeneous DINO/OpenCLIP pair is important because it provides a plausible source of complementary errors, but **heterogeneous foundation models are the experimental setting, not by themselves the algorithmic novelty**.

The central methodological claim should be:

> Training the failure-risk estimator on **domain-level pseudo-shifts** generated by source-domain cross-fitting transfers better to unseen target domains than conventional sample-level OOF risk training.

That is the cleanest and most defensible paper story.

---

# 2. Recommended paper story

The paper should have only **two core research questions**.

## RQ1 — Main methodological contribution

> Does domain-level source cross-fitting provide better failure-risk supervision for unseen-domain selective FAS than matched sample-level OOF training?

For fixed final heterogeneous classifier \(g_{DV}\), compare:

\[
r^{domain}_{DVd}
\]

against:

\[
r^{sample}_{DVd}.
\]

Primary contrast:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{OOF}
=
AP(e_{DV},r^{domain}_{DVd})
-
AP(e_{DV},r^{sample}_{DVd})
}
\]

where the final classifier predictions and error labels are identical.

This is the cleanest scientific experiment in the whole dossier.

It isolates the thing that is actually new:

\[
\boxed{
\text{how source failure supervision is generated}
}
\]

rather than confounding it with classifier accuracy.

## RQ2 — Why heterogeneous foundations are useful

> Does the studied DINOv2-Reg + OpenCLIP pair provide a better selective FAS system than a matched same-family DINOv2-Reg + DINOv2 pair?

This should be framed as an **empirical system comparison**, not as proof that heterogeneous pretraining causally creates diversity.

Compare:

\[
\text{DINO-Reg + OpenCLIP}
\]

against:

\[
\text{DINO-Reg + plain DINOv2}
\]

with matched source data, calibration, domain-OOF risk estimation, fusion rule, selective gate, and transaction accounting.

The claim is:

> Under this protocol, the heterogeneous pair provides more useful transferable failure information / selective utility than the matched same-family pair.

Do **not** claim:

> heterogeneous pretraining causes the gain.

That stronger mechanistic claim is not identified by the proposed experiments.

# 3. What should NOT be a core research question

### Explicit disagreement

\[
d_{abs}=|\widehat p_D-\widehat p_V|
\]

is a feature-attribution study.

If:

\[
\Delta_{dis}>0,
\]

then disagreement can enter the title or contribution list.

If not, the paper still survives.

Therefore explicit disagreement should **not** determine whether the main paper exists.

### Fusion rescue

Whether calibrated averaging improves raw classification is interesting, but it is not required for the failure-risk contribution.

Keep fusion rescue as a separate classifier result.

### Conditional routing

Routing is deployment engineering.

It can strengthen the discussion, but it should not be required for the Q3 paper.

If time becomes limited, routing is the first major experiment I would remove.

### SiW-M attack shift

Attack-shift validation is useful, but it should be a secondary extension.

The core paper can already be coherent around strict four-fold MICO domain generalization.

If SiW-M access, protocol reconstruction, or event counts become problematic, do not let this block the first paper.

# 4. The novelty should be stated more sharply

The paper is **not novel because of**:

- DINOv2-Reg;
- OpenCLIP;
- probability averaging;
- logistic regression;
- disagreement;
- abstention;
- confidence estimation in general.

The candidate novelty is the following combination:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{independently trained heterogeneous foundation predictors}\\
+\\
\text{domain-level source OOF error supervision}\\
+\\
\text{target-blind failure-risk estimation}\\
+\\
\text{security-aware selective PAD evaluation}
\end{array}
}
\]

More specifically:

> Instead of learning failure confidence from random/sample-level OOF predictions, construct failure supervision from **leave-one-source-domain-out predictors**, so that the risk estimator is explicitly trained on source pseudo-domain shifts resembling deployment shift.

That sentence should appear very early in the manuscript.

# 5. The method needs one concise named object

The current dossier is methodologically careful, but a paper cannot read like a collection of protocol safeguards.

The manuscript needs one compact method abstraction.

For example:

## Domain-OOF Failure Risk Estimation

Given source domains:

\[
\mathcal S=\{S_1,\ldots,S_K\},
\]

for each source domain \(S_k\):

1. train the FAS branches on \(\mathcal S\setminus S_k\);
2. calibrate them only on the allowed remainder;
3. predict \(S_k\);
4. construct
   \[
   z_i=[\widehat p_D,\widehat p_V,d,q];
   \]
5. assign
   \[
   e_i=\mathbf 1[g(x_i)\neq y_i].
   \]

Then train:

\[
r_\theta(z)\approx P(e=1\mid z)
\]

using the union of held-out-domain records.

At test time, train the final branches on all allowed source domains and use the frozen risk estimator on the unseen target.

That is the method.

Everything else should be clearly labeled as calibration detail, baseline, ablation, or deployment analysis.

---

# 6. Q3 novelty risk: the method is deliberately simple

A reviewer may say:

> You are just using leave-one-domain-out predictions to train a logistic confidence model.

That criticism is predictable.

The response cannot be that the pipeline is complicated.

The response must be empirical:

1. sample-OOF is carefully matched;
2. domain-OOF gives a repeatable improvement under unseen targets;
3. the improvement persists against MSP/entropy, operational margin, Mahalanobis confidence, and capacity-matched learned-risk baselines;
4. the resulting gate improves the security-usability trade-off;
5. the effect is stronger/useful with heterogeneous foundations than with same-family foundations.

If those results are strong, the simplicity is acceptable for a Q3 empirical-method paper.

If those results are weak, adding a fancy module later will probably not rescue the fundamental story.

# 7. Title recommendation

Current title:

This is defensible.

A slightly sharper title would be:

> **Source-Domain Cross-Fitted Failure-Risk Estimation for Selective Face Presentation Attack Detection**

Advantages:

- places the actual method in the title;
- avoids making heterogeneous foundation models sound like the novelty;
- survives even if the same-family comparison is less impressive than expected.

If RQ2 is strong, an alternative is:

> **Source-Domain Cross-Fitted Failure-Risk Estimation with Heterogeneous Foundation Models for Selective Face PAD**

Do **not** put `Disagreement` in the main title before \(\Delta_{dis}\) is proven.

# 8. The related-work table is still missing important 2026 neighbors

The 2026 VLM/DG-FAS neighborhood is denser than the current table suggests.

At minimum add verified discussion of:

- **AIM-FAS**, Pattern Recognition 2026;
- **DGPDL**, IEEE TPAMI 2026;
- **CLIP-SA**, IEEE Transactions on Multimedia 2026.

These works matter because they make:

> CLIP/VLM for cross-domain FAS

clearly non-novel.

However, their primary mechanisms are different.

### AIM-FAS
Adapts a vision-language model for generalizable FAS and addresses imbalance.

### DGPDL
Learns domain-guided prompt distributions to improve generalization.

### CLIP-SA
Uses CLIP-guided semantic alignment for generalizable FAS.

Therefore the paper should explicitly say:

> We do not adapt prompts or align source-domain representations to improve the classifier. We keep predictors independent and study whether source-domain pseudo-shifts can supervise failure-risk transfer to unseen domains.

---

# 9. RPSR-FAS is probably the closest 2026 conceptual threat

RPSR-FAS explicitly combines VLM semantics, cross-domain FAS, and reliability learning.

The distinction needs to be extremely explicit.

## RPSR-FAS

Its reliability estimate is used inside training to weight / reorganize supervision and improve the FAS representation/classifier.

## Proposed work

The proposed failure-risk estimator is:

- post-predictive;
- trained from held-out-domain prediction failures;
- source-only;
- evaluated as a selector/abstention mechanism on unseen target domains;
- intentionally preserves independent DINO/VLM errors.

A good differentiating sentence is:

> Unlike reliability-aware FAS methods that use reliability to improve classifier training, we use source-domain held-out prediction failures as supervision for a separate target-blind failure-risk model.

---

# 10. CA-FAS is the closest reliability baseline and must be taken seriously

CA-FAS already studies confidence, capability boundaries, unfamiliar domains/attacks, and rejection of low-confidence samples.

Its confidence uses Gaussian class modeling and Mahalanobis distance, with confidence-aware representation optimization.

Therefore merely showing that the method rejects uncertain cases is not novel.

The paper needs to demonstrate that:

\[
\text{domain-OOF failure supervision}
\]

outperforms or complements representation-space confidence such as Mahalanobis confidence.

Keep CA-FAS as a first-class competitor, not a minor baseline mention.

---

# 11. The paper should focus on domain shift first

For a first Q3 paper, use four-fold MICO as the core protocol.

For each target:

\[
3\ source\ domains\rightarrow1\ untouched\ target.
\]

This directly supports RQ1.

SiW-M should be external validation only if access and protocol quality are good.

Do not make the paper's title or abstract depend on SiW-M unless those results are actually completed.

# 13. RQ1 is currently stronger than RQ2 — and that is good

Earlier versions effectively asked whether DINO and VLM are complementary.

That is vulnerable because complementarity itself is not novel.

Now RQ1 asks whether domain-level cross-fitted failure supervision transfers better.

That is a much better paper question.

Even if RQ2 produces only modest advantage over DINO+DINO, RQ1 can still support a useful paper.

In that case, title and abstract should emphasize:

\[
\text{source-domain cross-fitted failure-risk estimation}
\]

and describe heterogeneous foundations as the primary instantiation, not as the central novelty.

# 14. One conceptual issue remains in RQ1 wording

RQ1 currently contains both failure ranking and selective security-usability.

A cleaner narrative is:

\[
\text{domain-OOF}
\rightarrow
\text{better error ranking}
\rightarrow
\text{better source-selected selective trade-off}.
\]

Therefore:

### Primary endpoint

\[
AP_{error}.
\]

### Operational validation

Selective security-usability curves and end-to-end metrics.

Do not define RQ1 success as a loose conjunction of every metric.

If AP improves but one arbitrary operating point does not, that should not automatically falsify the risk-transfer mechanism.

Likewise, good AP with no useful selective trade-off weakens the practical claim but not necessarily the risk-ranking result.

---

# 15. Do not oversell "foundation models"

The experiments test one concrete pair:

DINOv2\text{-Reg}+OpenCLIP\ ViT-B/16.
\]

Therefore use wording such as:

> a heterogeneous foundation-model pair

or:

> independently pretrained visual and vision-language foundation predictors

rather than implying universality across arbitrary foundation models.

# 16. The same-family control is important but should not dominate the paper

The DINO-Reg + DINO-plain control is necessary to prevent the criticism that any two models would work.

But it should support the narrative, not become a second research project.

Do not add ResNet ensemble, Swin ensemble, multiple CLIP variants, or multiple DINO scales unless reviewers later demand them.

For a Q3 paper, one strong matched same-family control is enough if it is genuinely fair.

# 17. Recommended contribution list

Use only three contributions.

### Contribution 1

A source-domain cross-fitted failure-risk estimation framework for selective single-image face PAD, where failure supervision is generated from leave-one-source-domain-out predictors rather than conventional random/sample-level OOF predictions.

### Contribution 2

A heterogeneous DINOv2-Reg/OpenCLIP instantiation that preserves independent visual and semantic predictors and evaluates whether their combined outputs provide transferable failure information beyond single-branch and matched same-family controls.

### Contribution 3

A strict target-blind evaluation protocol connecting failure ranking to biometric security/usability through source-selected selective policies, explicit APCER/BPCER/coverage accounting, and four-fold MICO evaluation.

Do **not** list disagreement as a contribution unless the ablation passes.

Do **not** list routing as a contribution in the first submission unless it is actually run and strong.

# 18. Recommended abstract-level hypothesis

> Conventional confidence estimation is usually learned from in-distribution or sample-level held-out predictions, which may not reflect the failure patterns encountered under cross-domain face PAD. We instead generate failure supervision from leave-one-source-domain-out predictors and train a source-only risk estimator over independently pretrained DINOv2 and OpenCLIP outputs. We test whether this domain-level pseudo-shift supervision improves error ranking and selective security-usability on unseen target domains.

That is much clearer than a story centered on DINO/VLM disagreement.

# 19. Publication assessment

## For a Q3 journal

The plan is **plausible and coherent** if experiments show:

1. domain-OOF risk training reliably beats matched sample-OOF;
2. gains hold across most/all MICO folds;
3. gains are not explained by a trivial operational-margin baseline;
4. selective gating yields a useful source-selected security-usability advantage;
5. heterogeneous pair is at least competitive with, preferably better than, the same-family matched system;
6. CA-FAS / confidence-aware baselines are handled fairly.

This is enough for a solid empirical-method paper.

## For Q1 / strong top conference

The current method would probably be considered algorithmically light.

That is acceptable because Q3 is the current objective.

Do not destroy a coherent Q3 paper by adding unnecessary architectural novelty simply to imitate a Q1 paper.
